title: "Obama's Audacity"
date: "2026-02-07T11:01:05"
---
Up until my early 20s, I pretty much held up two middle fingers to American politics. I was always disillusioned and disappointed by the grandstanding, narcissistic “leaders” whom we were supposed to look up to for their dedicated servitude and sacrifice. It was obviously just a merry-go-round of cults of personality, and those personalities were almost all incredibly awful when the masks came off.

In 2005, I traveled to Lincolnshire, IL for two weeks to train for my new (first real) job. There was a local buzz around the Senate campaign of this young, confident upstart politician named Obama. People were talking about him during lunch with genuine excitement, which intrigued me. I read about him in the local news and did some digging online, and he seemed more legit than most, but I was, of course, dubious of someone who seemed too good to be true. He won that election, and I went back into my “whatever” political cocoon.

Fast forward to the 2008 presidential election cycle. Obama emerged as a serious contender, and I followed along closely. After reading The Audacity of Hope, I was all in. This man was the real deal. A true American success story, but more importantly, he was a realist about a path forward for America that didn’t involve aggrandizing a false nostalgia or an overly-ambitious utopian vision. His pragmatic assessment of where this country has been and how to best move it forward was honest and simple. In the spirit of Lincoln, it was to focus on what united us over what divided us, and to elevate the “better angels of our nature.” More simply put, don’t lose hope, and focus on making progress wherever possible, even when the deck seems stacked against it. I could go on and on, but I’ll just say that his articulation of a realistic, positive political future changed me forever. It made me less jaded, less cynical, and more willing to participate in a society that, even though deeply flawed in so many ways, offered various opportunities for better lives for so many.

The Audacity of Hope… what a phrase… what an idea… what a dogma of possibility. I just feel like this was especially important for me to revisit and write about during Black History Month, and, of course, as a direct response to the vile, racist post from the current POTUS.

I generally don’t get into politics online. It isn’t because I’m hiding something, or that I don’t care. It is mainly because I don’t see social media as a productive forum for instigating any real change, and I get no real personal satisfaction or catharsis from screaming into an echo chamber, especially one that lines the pockets of greedy billionaires in direct proportion to our outraged engagement with their platforms. This isn’t me saying that those of you who do so are doing anything wrong at all. It’s just not for me.

I will just say this, to perhaps remove any doubt that the people I care about may have about my political views: I am unequivocally opposed to regressive politics, and I will never support those who believe that strength is rooted in callousness, as opposed to charity and care for all human beings. Hold on hope.
